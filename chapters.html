What You'll Learn

11 chapters covering the frameworks you need to transform quality from firefighting to strategic advantage.

The Complete Roadmap

Each chapter builds on the last, taking you from understanding the true cost of poor quality to implementing world-class prevention systems. Along the way, you'll get downloadable templates, real-world examples, and practical guidance you can use immediately.

1

The Quality Reckoning

Meet Kristina Valdez on her first day as Quality Director at Aegis Aviation—a mid-market aerospace manufacturer drowning in scrap, rework, and customer complaints. The company culture treats quality as the enemy, and she has 90 days to turn it around or be replaced.

Key Takeaways:

  • • Why quality positioned as "bad guy" destroys companies
  • • The real consequences of reactive firefighting culture
  • • How to diagnose your current quality maturity level
2

The Hidden Factory

Kristina discovers that Aegis is spending 25% of revenue on poor quality—scrap, rework, warranty claims, and expedited freight. But the CFO doesn't see it because COPQ (Cost of Poor Quality) isn't tracked anywhere in the accounting system. She must make the invisible visible.

Key Takeaways:

  • • How to calculate and track Cost of Poor Quality (COPQ)
  • • The 1:10:100 rule—why prevention is always cheaper
  • • Making the business case for quality investment to your CFO
3

Design for Manufacturing Excellence (DFMEA)

Engineering releases a new design without considering manufacturability. Production can't build it to print. Quality gets blamed. Kristina introduces DFMEA (Design Failure Mode Effects Analysis) to prevent design-related failures before manufacturing ever starts.

Key Takeaways:

  • • Step-by-step DFMEA process that actually works
  • • How to calculate Risk Priority Numbers (RPN) correctly
  • • Getting engineering buy-in for design prevention
4

Building It Right (PFMEA)

Even with a manufacturable design, production processes can still fail. A critical heat-treat process keeps producing out-of-spec parts, causing a $200K scrap event. Kristina implements PFMEA (Process Failure Mode Effects Analysis) to prevent process failures before they create defects.

Key Takeaways:

  • • PFMEA methodology for manufacturing processes
  • • Identifying critical control points and mistake-proofing
  • • How PFMEA and control plans work together
5

Your Supply Chain Is Your Problem

A low-cost supplier ships an entire lot of defective castings. Production uses them (behind schedule). Customer returns flood in six months later. Kristina realizes that supplier quality management isn't optional—your supply chain quality IS your quality.

Key Takeaways:

  • • How to qualify and audit suppliers effectively
  • • Incoming inspection strategies that actually work
  • • Building supplier partnerships vs. adversarial relationships
6

Control Plans That Actually Control

Production has procedures for everything, but defects still escape. The problem? Control plans are just checkbox compliance documents that nobody uses. Kristina transforms them into living tools that actually prevent defects at critical control points.

Key Takeaways:

  • • Creating control plans that operators actually use
  • • Linking PFMEA risk to control plan rigor
  • • Statistical Process Control (SPC) for critical parameters
7

When Prevention Fails: CAPA Done Right

Even with great prevention systems, failures will happen. The question is: do you learn from them? Aegis has hundreds of "corrective actions" in the system, but the same failures keep repeating. Kristina implements a CAPA (Corrective and Preventive Action) system that actually closes the loop.

Key Takeaways:

  • • Root cause analysis techniques that find real causes
  • • The difference between containment and corrective action
  • • How to verify effectiveness and prevent recurrence
8

Making Data Work for You

Aegis collects mountains of quality data but makes few decisions from it. Inspection reports, gage R&R studies, SPC charts—all filed away and ignored. Kristina shows how to turn quality data into actionable intelligence that drives continuous improvement.

Key Takeaways:

  • • Key quality metrics that actually drive behavior
  • • How to present data to executives who don't care about statistics
  • • Building dashboards that trigger action, not just reporting
9

Building a Quality Culture

All the frameworks in the world don't work if your culture treats quality as someone else's problem. Production needs to own quality. Engineering needs to design it in. Leadership needs to invest in prevention. Kristina works to transform Aegis's culture from "us vs. quality" to "quality is everyone's job."

Key Takeaways:

  • • How to change culture when you're not the CEO
  • • Training programs that create quality ownership
  • • Metrics and incentives that reinforce prevention behaviors
10

Quality as Competitive Advantage

Aegis wins a major contract they wouldn't have been competitive for a year ago—because their quality reputation improved so dramatically. Kristina demonstrates that quality isn't just cost reduction; it's revenue generation. Customers pay premiums for reliability, and competitors can't easily copy a mature quality system.

Key Takeaways:

  • • How to position quality as a sales advantage
  • • Building customer confidence through transparency
  • • Using quality certifications (AS9100, ISO 9001) strategically
11

Your 90-Day Quality Transformation Plan

You've seen Kristina's journey. Now it's time for yours. This final chapter provides a practical 90-day roadmap for transforming your quality system—what to do in Week 1, Month 1, Quarter 1, and Year 1. Prioritized by impact and feasibility, so you can get quick wins while building long-term capability.

Key Takeaways:

  • • Week 1: Assess current state and calculate COPQ
  • • Month 1: Quick wins that build credibility
  • • Quarter 1: Implement DFMEA/PFMEA for highest-risk products
  • • Year 1: Full quality system transformation roadmap
